I am Chief Marketing Officer at DUSK, a UK digital-first homeware retailer, leading the marketing, ecommerce and technology function from the senior leadership team.

My role is to set the commercial direction for the function, build the operating model, and make sure marketing, ecommerce and technology work as one integrated system rather than as independent channels. The team I lead covers full-funnel marketing, ecommerce trading and CRO, CRM and lifecycle, and the technology and data infrastructure underneath it.

The work underway at DUSK is a shift from a performance-heavy acquisition model into a more balanced demand engine - brand, mid-funnel, CRM and loyalty operating together. In practice that means phasing media reallocation against clear efficiency guardrails, rebuilding measurement so capital allocation is informed by incrementality rather than last-click, launching a structured loyalty and lifecycle programme, and professionalising the marketing organisation around commercial outcomes. The team are the people doing this - my job is to frame the problem, design the operating model, and clear the path.

I think of marketing as a commercial discipline, not a creative one. Brand is valuable because it increases the productivity of performance. Measurement matters because it drives behaviour. Teams compound when structure, accountability and trust are clear.

Before DUSK, I have worked in retail ecommerce and digital growth since 2015, across high-growth and scale-up environments. Earlier in my career I led IT infrastructure in the public sector, which still informs how I think about the technology and data layer underneath marketing.

If you are working on the brand–performance integration problem, full-funnel measurement, or scaling marketing in a high-growth business, happy to compare notes.

03 Writing

A note on what I’m working on.

Featured · Apr 2026

Three years ago I stopped thinking about brand and performance as separate budgets.

The instinct to split marketing into “brand spend” and “performance spend” is one of the most expensive habits in UK retail right now. It makes for tidy governance, clean P&L reporting, and poor capital allocation.

Here is what actually happens when brand is run as a parallel line item.

Performance teams get measured on last-click ROAS and are pushed to over-invest in the channels that harvest branded demand - branded search, retargeting, anything close to the bottom of the funnel. MER looks healthy. Growth stalls.

Brand teams get measured on awareness trackers and are pushed to defend creative expression against commercial logic. The aesthetic improves. The performance team rolls its eyes.

Nobody owns the productivity of the system.

Brand is valuable because it increases the productivity of performance, not because it looks nice.

The shift we are making is simple to describe and hard to execute: every pound spent on brand is an investment in the CTR, CVR, blended CAC and repeat rate of the pounds spent downstream. Measurement has to prove that or kill it. Capital allocation has to follow the measurement.

When this is set up properly, the argument about the “right split” disappears. You stop asking “how much brand, how much performance” and start asking “what is the marginal productivity of the next pound”. Two budgets become one system.

It takes longer than it should. It costs political capital. It is worth it.
